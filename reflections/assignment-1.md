# Assignment 1 reflection

The real breakthrough was realizing two different problems were actually the
same problem.

On the creative side, I started with what I liked most, music, and my first
instinct was to show off: maj7 chords, flat fives, all the interesting stuff.
But that would've made anyone without a music background feel like they
didn't have a clue what was going on. So I scoped it down to the basic seven
chords instead, and that same instinct carried into the design: instead of
dropping a circle-of-fifths key selector onto the page with no explanation, I
had to build up to it first, showing why some keys relate to each other
before asking the reader to trust a shape they'd never seen.

On the technical side, the same kind of thinking showed up again. Instead of
writing out 84 chords by hand for every key, I found the one pattern, a fixed
scale shape plus a fixed sequence of chord types, that generates all of them.
Getting the audio to sound right needed the same move: not just picking the
right notes, but stacking them in the right order so a chord actually sounds
like a chord instead of a jumble.

That's what I want to carry forward as a developer: look for the underlying
pattern, whether it's in the notes or in the reader's understanding, before
writing anything down.
